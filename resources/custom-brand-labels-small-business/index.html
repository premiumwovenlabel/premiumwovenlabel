Custom brand labels for small businesses

How small and handmade businesses approach their first label order — what to decide, what to skip for now, and how to keep it simple.

First label orders go wrong in predictable ways: too many decisions made at once, a design that looks fine on screen and disappears in thread, or a quantity picked before the brand knows what it needs. Here’s a simpler way through it.

Start with one label

You don’t need a full label programme to look professional. One well-made brand label in the neck or the side seam does more for how your product is received than four mediocre ones ordered in a hurry.

Decide which single label your customer is most likely to see, and order that one properly. Size, care and hang tags can follow once you know how the first one turned out.

The decisions that matter

A label order comes down to a handful of choices, and most of them are easier than they sound:

  • Label type — brand label, size label, care label, hang tag.
  • Size — driven by where it sits and how much your artwork contains.
  • Shape — rectangular covers most needs; square and custom shapes are available.
  • Fold — Straight Cut, Centre Fold, End Fold, Mitre Fold or Manhattan Fold.
  • Thread colours — a focused palette keeps the weave clean.
  • Quantity — minimum order quantities apply and depend on the label type.

If you’re unsure on any of these, say so when you ask for a quote. It’s a normal part of the conversation and it’s faster than guessing.

What to skip for now

Small businesses often overthink the parts that don’t change the outcome. A few things you can safely put aside on a first order:

  • Custom shapes — lovely, but a clean rectangle is what most established brands use.
  • A large colour palette — two or three colours usually look sharper than six.
  • Every piece of text you own — the tagline and the website address can go on the hang tag or nowhere at all.
  • Matching every label type immediately — plan the look once, order the pieces as you need them.

If you don’t have a designer

Plenty of small brands don’t, and it isn’t a blocker. Send whatever version of your logo you have — the biggest, cleanest file you own — and we’ll tell you whether it works at label size and what would need simplifying if it doesn’t.

If all you have is your brand name, a woven label with just the name set cleanly in thread is a perfectly legitimate place to start. Our logo preparation guide covers what to check before you send anything.

Thinking about quantity

Minimum order quantities apply and vary by label type, so the practical approach is to tell us roughly how much you produce and let us tell you what’s workable rather than guessing a number first.

The thing to weigh up is how settled your branding is. If your logo is likely to change in the next few months, order what covers the near term. If it’s fixed, a larger run is generally more economical per label — we’ll give you the actual figures when we quote.

Handmade and small-batch makers

If you sew, knit, crochet or make by hand, a woven label is often the single thing that shifts a piece from “homemade” to “made by a brand” in a customer’s mind. The work is already there; the label just stops it being mistaken for something less considered.

Small makers usually do well with a simple centre fold brand label in one or two colours — quick to sew in, durable through washing, and instantly recognisable across everything you make.

What to do next

  1. Pick the one label your customer will see.
  2. Print your logo at the label’s real size and check it still reads.
  3. Note roughly how many you need and by when.
  4. Send all of that on WhatsApp and ask for a quote.

There’s no cost to asking and no obligation after. If something about your artwork or quantity won’t work, we’ll tell you before you spend anything.
